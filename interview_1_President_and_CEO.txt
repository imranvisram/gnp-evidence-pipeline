GNP FOUNDATION — INTERVIEW NOTES (1 of 5) | President & CEO

PAIN POINTS
- We just don't deliver well. The hierarchy causes leadership to struggle with how to get approvals
- No one knows how to work faster — I'm working to get rid of a lot of these approvals
- We don't have an archaic workforce anymore — young people are energized by the mission but are quashed by the bureaucracy
- "I started 30 years ago as a controller, so a lot of the bureaucracy is my responsibility"
- I want titles to be less important than getting the work done
- Worked across many audit teams, we could do that here to tap into the talent that each project needs

STRATEGY
- Lots of attention on outcomes — evidence and show your impact
- A lot of the institutions we support will be in need of both funding and strategic support
- We can offer responsive funding — but not our primary focus. "My President's Discretionary Fund"
- Grantees want to see more of us, we are very slow, not responsive and are taking way too long — they want our intellect as well as our resources, to sit down and have a strategy conversation with them

STRUCTURE BARRIERS
- "Our current structure relies too much on 'one person at the top having the answer'"
- Sometimes that person at the top is overriding what the community wants — want to get rid of this pyramid

LEADERSHIP
- All of our leaders have a coach, moving away from 'command and control'
- Making a big investment here over the past year
- Started with Governance, moved onto Leadership and needed to tackle structure as part of that

CURRENT STATE CHALLENGES
- Barriers to achieving the mission? We're getting better but people work within their departments. "The Community team won't speak to the Early Childhood Education Team"
- We have a lot of that happening — a lot of structural barriers dependent on your title and who your boss is
- PCKA — Policy, Communications, Knowledge and Analytics: producing the evidence that drives national policy conversations
- Because they're stuck within that unit, they're making up their own work
- Early Childhood Education should be driving the policy changes and Policy people should be supporting it
- Policy should be in service to the programs and programs should be inviting in the policy people
- Networked teams should be always fluid
- We are trying to structure ourselves to become in the top quartile of our peers
- "As an organization we are very resistant to change — no one wants to change what they do or how they do it. Inertia is a dominating force and it takes a lot for us to break out of old ways of working"

SKILL GAPS
- Program Specialist: We used to have 1:1 support for Program Officers (now it's 1:2, plus an assistant for each area and a budget assistant); we should eventually be able to stop hiring these old traditional FTEs
- We need people who are generalists who can work across communities — people who can work with and energize people
- Some of our people are NOT good at knowing how to move things and make things work
- Our leaders have evolved from program officers, but they can't lead people

STRENGTHS
- Core values: should be preserved
- People are hungry for something better
- Diversity in our people — we have embraced core values for both our Board and staff

RISKS
- Would be interested in piloting to ensure it works
- Could lose the connection to our grantees — become out of touch
- Need to bring people along and communicate effectively
- Yes, am open to significant change at the executive table
