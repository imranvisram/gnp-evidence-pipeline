GNP FOUNDATION — INTERVIEW NOTES (5 of 5) | Project Manager

BURNING PLATFORM
- CEO is now in a position to implement the changes she has always wanted
- Nearing the end of finalizing our strategy
- Each team has its own strategic planning process — silos off doing their own thing

HOPES & FEARS
- Paranoia — hoping to see the org become less stuck in its ways
- Painful slow decision-making process
- A lot of things go up to the CEO & Executive Council, they may postpone a decision to the next meeting two weeks away
- Monthly Board Meeting — impacts Executive Council, but not the full org — significant time suck

DECISION MAKING — SUCCESSES
- We had 8 different groups writing strategy papers
- Agreed on timeframe and template, then teams were allowed to deploy their own plan

FUTURE STRATEGY
- CEO is very focused on results and outcome-based metrics
- Likely no additional communities to expand into — we are committed to those geographies for a generation (20-25 years)
- How did these priorities evolve? Planning process under previous CEO. Chose areas with lowest community health/poverty scores both domestic and internationally

FUTURE DEMANDS
- Every two years we do a staff perception survey and a grantee perception survey — low grantee/customer satisfaction
- Grantees (who are our customers) want to see grants processed faster

LEADERSHIP ALIGNMENT
- Leaders are a lot more aware of what each other are doing, but still operate very much in silos
- Largest internal barrier is internal communications — that team has been restructured a few times
- Messaging to staff and grantees is lacking
- "Pretty soon 50 people start hearing about this project and in lieu of any clear communications, the rumor mill starts"

HANDOFFS AND SILOS
- The organization is totally relationship based
- National work led by one VP vs. Program work led by a different VP
- There are high tensions over where dollars are spent, and getting mindshare of the leadership team
- Chief Policy Officer oversees communications, policy and analytics
- Grant Management Department — whether it be lobbying, effectiveness, or tax implications, this group can hold up the grantmaking process, which frustrates the program officers
- Quality & Org. Effectiveness — seen as the new department holding other teams accountable, a structure that could cause tensions
- "Right now there's a lot of back and forth before we can make a decision, the Family Health Team would have to work with the Geography, would have to loop in their supervisors, grant management department, legal team for advice — lots of touchpoints across the organization"

SKILL GAPS
- We may hire someone who knows great food policy, but who isn't a great communicator or presenter
- Phase 2 & 3 will be key to identifying skill gaps

EXEC COUNCIL DECISION MAKING
- Example: Pulse Questions — the number of times the questions were reviewed by Core Team and Exec Council in a very circular way

STRENGTHS
- Surveys tell us — people love the diversity and knowledge and experiences of their colleagues, the top positive is always peers
- Legacy is very important — incl. vision, mission and values

RISKS
- Not communicating well with staff — change management is going to be critical, people are going to be very sensitive talking about how and who people work with
- Equity — top people often get pulled onto special projects, how do you pull everyone into this networked team?
- Program officers — matrixed — time spent on two teams, how do I split my time? How do my two supervisors split the time?
