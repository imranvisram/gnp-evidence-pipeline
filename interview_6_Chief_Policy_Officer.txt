GNP FOUNDATION — INTERVIEW NOTES (6 of 6) | Chief Policy Officer

FRAMING
- Wants to be on record that the diagnosis so far is "a structure answer to a talent problem"
- Has been at GNP 14 years, longest tenure on the executive team
- "I am not against change. I am against changing the one part of this place that currently works"

ON THE CURRENT STRUCTURE
- PCKA produces the evidence base — national policy work has a 3-4 year horizon, not a sprint
- Pushes back on the idea that policy exists to serve programs
- "If policy becomes a service desk for the program teams, we stop setting the agenda and start taking orders"
- Networked teams sound fine for grantmaking; unclear how they work for longitudinal research

ON THE GRANTEE SATISFACTION DATA
- Thinks the drop is partly a measurement artifact — survey instrument was revised in 2024
- Cannot say how much of the decline is real vs. instrument change — "no one has gone back to check"
- Grantee mix has also shifted toward smaller community orgs with less capacity to navigate us
- Agrees the direction is bad regardless

ON DECISION MAKING
- Agrees decisions take too long, disagrees on the cause
- "It isn't the org chart. It's that nobody is willing to be the one who says no"
- Escalation is a symptom of people protecting themselves, not of hierarchy
- Has seen three restructures here; none of them changed the speed of anything

THE INCUMBENT VENDOR
- Their structure work last year recommended consolidating the four program areas into two portfolios
- Also recommended keeping PCKA as a standalone function reporting to the CEO
- Report was circulated to Executive Council in November, never formally adopted or rejected
- "It is sitting in a drawer and now a second firm is being asked the same question"

ON THE CEO
- Respects her, says the ambition is right
- Worries the timeline is being set by her retirement horizon rather than the organization's readiness
- Notes she has asked for a pilot, which he reads as a good sign

WHAT HE WOULD MEASURE
- Time from grant application to decision, not to disbursement — disbursement delays are finance, not program
- Whether policy outputs get cited in national conversations
- Staff turnover in the first two years of tenure specifically

STRENGTHS
- The evidence function is genuinely respected externally
- Convening power — "people take our calls"

RISKS
- Losing institutional memory if the reorg pushes out long-tenured staff
- Generalists cannot do what specialists do in policy work
- "You can teach a specialist to collaborate. You cannot teach a generalist twenty years of a policy file"
