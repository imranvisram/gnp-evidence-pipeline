GNP FOUNDATION — INTERVIEW NOTES (2 of 5) | Chief Operating Officer

PAIN POINTS — WHY THIS? WHY NOW?
- Is very implementation focused
- If we want to operate differently, we can't do so in the old structure — there is a traditional hierarchy built into the organization
- HR drove a lot of past policymaking (structure, comp, leadership models)

EVOLUTION OF ORG
- We used to hire program experts with deep knowledge (Academia model)
- Shifted towards generalists under single purpose, more of a corporate structure — "braided efforts to execute"
- Happening over the last decade, previous CEO mobilized people around levers of change, but didn't carry that through to the structure
- The concept of cross-functional ways of working isn't new to us, that's how we operate externally but not how we operate internally
- "Health would show up in New Mexico and Education would show up in NM. So there wasn't a unified GNP foundation to the community there, there were multiple GNP Foundations"
- "How do we show up to our grantees to drive systemic change?"

READINESS
- Thirds — a third are committed to the need for change, a third are willing and open, a third will be resistors — needing a lot of work (some know what the change implies and won't like it)
- For resistors, this may revoke the social contract that they were brought here to do
- Some will just lack the capacity and skills to function as part of a multi-disciplinary team
- This will be a huge change for frontline staff — it's a bigger deal than we realize

LEADERSHIP WORK
- We are committed to the success of CEO and the organization
- BUT until we know what shifting roles and duties looks like, people won't be aligned
- We need people or relationships with people who have varying skill / knowledge sets vs. deep expertise in one area

HANDOFFS & WORKFLOWS
- Health and Wellness team, and Economic Security team all feed into the school experience
- May need to bring in specialists and experts as needed — cross-functional teams would need to be empowered to pull in the resources to get it done
- Old paradigm: who has lead accountability for Community team? Regional lead, education expert, specialist in community engagement?
- We used to have teams solely on cities — Community teams used to hire consultants rather than asking their colleagues
- There are skill gaps that show up like "The ability to know what resources you need and when to engage them"
- E.g. If we need the contract services team to rush deliver something, the contracting person gets blindsided because they weren't at the table and coordinated at the outset
- Need to be aided by planfulness and proactiveness — people didn't know how to make workplans when I arrived

EXAMPLES
- CEO often has to sign off on something that has already been approved (e.g. vendor contracts or vacation approvals shouldn't go up to the CEO)
- Our bureaucracy is a major time suck — need to start over with simplifying in mind to get to best outcome
- Very time consuming — and even with this we sometimes fail to make and communicate decisions
- Complicated by lack of prioritizing… too many needs, projects, requests

EXPECTATIONS GAME
- Willingness to work through an example, people would start to fidget if we don't talk about the implementation
- What are the critical success factors to create culture change? Implementation and launch?
- People will want to learn how other organizations have tackled these issues
